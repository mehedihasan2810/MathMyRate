# Keyword research

Research date: 2026-09-16. This is a snapshot. Search behavior and page-one
results change. Repeat the checks before each large content batch, and replace
estimates with Search Console data after launch.

## Summary

1. **For creator platforms, people type "X fees" more than "X fee
   calculator".** Patreon, Substack, Skool, Teachable, Podia, Payhip,
   Kickstarter, and Indiegogo show "fees", "pricing", or "how much does X
   charge" far more often; for Substack, Skool, Teachable, and Podia the
   calculator phrase did not appear at all. For Stripe, PayPal, Etsy, eBay,
   Upwork, and Fiverr, both forms are common. Titles and first paragraphs
   should carry the "fees" phrase. The H1 can stay a calculator name.
2. **Creator platform pages are the most winnable.** Page one for Patreon,
   Ko-fi, Substack, Payhip, Whop, Skool, Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Indiegogo, and
   Kickstarter is held by thin calculator sites, help pages, and blogs.
3. **Big marketplaces, Stripe, tax, and generic money calculators are hard.**
   Etsy, eBay, Amazon, "stripe fee calculator", "salary to hourly", "profit
   margin", and tax calculators belong to funded tools, big publishers, and
   long-standing calculator sites.
4. **The best new pages are reseller and payment-app fees.** Depop, Poshmark,
   Mercari, Facebook Marketplace, Venmo, and Cash App have strong question
   demand, weak page one, and ranking pages that disagree on the fees.
5. **Some existing pages target the wrong phrase.** "project rate calculator"
   is barely typed. "rate increase calculator" is typed, but for loan and
   percent increases, not freelance rates. "podia fee calculator" was not seen;
   "podia pricing" and "how much does podia cost" were.
6. **There is strong non-US demand.** "uk", "australia", "canada", and "india"
   versions are common for Stripe, PayPal, Etsy, eBay, and KDP. Every page is
   US-only today.
7. **No free source gives search volume.** Use Google Keyword Planner (list in
   [Next steps](#next-steps)) before choosing between gap pages, and Search
   Console after launch.

## Method

All sources were free and needed no account.

| Source                          | What it shows                                | Size                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Google autocomplete (`gl=us`)   | Phrases people type, in popularity order     | 3,091 lookups: each topic, plus "a" to "z" |
| Bing autocomplete (`mkt=en-US`) | A second set of phrases                      | 283 lookups                                |
| Autocomplete prefix test        | Which phrase form wins for the same platform | 100 phrases, typed one character at a time |
| Web search, page one            | Who ranks, and whether a calculator is there | 67 keywords, one search each               |
| Google Trends                   | Relative interest                            | Not available; see Limits                  |

Requests were sent one at a time with a pause. Nothing was blocked except
Google Trends. The autocomplete results gave about 11,600 phrases, of which
about 10,500 relate to fees, prices, rates, or comparisons.

"Score" in the tables below is the number of different lookups that returned a
phrase. A higher score means the phrase comes up for many partial searches. It
is a rough signal, not a volume.

## Limits

- **No search volume or ad value.** Autocomplete shows that a phrase is
  common, not how common. Competition ratings are judgments, not scores.
- **Google Trends was blocked.** Trends marked the automated browser as a
  scraper and returned "429 Too Many Requests" for its data. The links in
  [Next steps](#next-steps) can be opened by hand in a normal browser.
- **Request location.** Requests asked for US results, but some phrases
  ("paypal fee calculator usd to php", "india" versions) suggest the results
  still lean toward the requester's location. US results may differ slightly.
- **Page one was not Google.** The web search tool is US-only, shows no
  "People also ask", AI answers, or ads, and showed no Reddit threads. On
  Google, some "easier" ratings may be one step harder.
- **The prefix test has low resolution.** Almost every "X fees" phrase appears
  after typing the platform name plus "f", so it cannot rank platforms against
  each other. It only shows which phrase form wins for one platform.

Avoid for now:

- **Tax calculators** (freelance tax, self-employment tax): TurboTax, NerdWallet,
  and banks hold page one, and tax is a high-trust topic.
- **Amazon seller fees:** Amazon's own calculator and Helium 10 rank.
- **Profit margin, generic salary converters:** big calculator sites rank.
- **Zelle:** banks set the fees, and most transfers are free, so a calculator
  adds little.
- **Buy Me a Coffee:** demand is real and page one is weak, but its pages do
  not state how its fees combine (see the improvement plan).

## 4. Non-US demand

Country versions are common for the biggest platforms:

- Stripe: uk, australia, canada, nz, gst.
- PayPal: uk, australia, international, india, "usd to php".
- Etsy, eBay, Shopify, Depop: uk and australia.
- KDP: uk and india royalty calculators.
- Freelance: "freelance rate calculator uk" (score 10) and UK day rates.

A UK version of the Stripe, PayPal, Etsy, and eBay calculators would need
UK rates from each provider's UK pages, GBP, and VAT handling. It is a larger
project and should wait for Search Console data.

## 5. Competitors

- **payoutmath.com** is the closest match: US-focused, official sources,
  review dates, and many of the same platforms.
- A cluster of new creator-fee calculator sites targets the same list:
  creatordia.com, sublyna.com, transferfees.io, sellerfeescalculator.com,
  notiontour.com, feecalculator.us, knowyourcut.com, creatortake.com. Most are
  thin (400 to 500 words, no sources).
- **yotpo.com/tools** and **ebayfeescalculator.com** each appear in nine
  result sets. Several ebayfeescalculator.com pages still say "Updated Aug 2024".
- Most ranking titles include "2026". A dated title is the norm.
